As I approach 23, I find myself looking at love in friendships, relationships & me, through a clearer, more grounded lens. Here are 23 ideas on love that I’m ready to take a stand for.


# On Love

23 ideas across romantic love, friendship, family and self-love.

# I. Romantic Love


### 1. Love Is a Choice on a Random Tuesday

What would a Tuesday afternoon look like with them?

So if I had to write about my random Tuesday, it might look like this:

I spend the day working on architectural renders. In the evening, I get a message from him:

> "Found a book I think you'd like."

We meet up.

Maybe we grab food, walk around, talk about some building, some movie or some ridiculous friendship dilemma.

At some point, there's a hug, maybe a hand held absent-mindedly.

Nothing dramatic happens.

And I go home feeling:

> "My life is better because this person is in it."

Ironically, that's not very cinematic.

But most lasting love stories are built from a lot of Tuesdays, not from one perfect kiss where the leg pops.

## 2. Tolerate Uncertainty, But Never Accept Aimlessness

Life may be unpredictable, but intention matters. Build a life with someone with their own direction, ambitions and purpose, who also believes in yours and supports you while you're still figuring things out.

## 3. The Goal Is to Be Known, Not Just Chosen

Being chosen satisfies the ego; being deeply understood satisfies something much deeper. Find someone who notices how you want to be cared for and with whom you can show your unedited self.

## 4. Grand Gestures Must Honour the Quiet Days

Grand gestures should elevate the ordinary, not compensate for the absence of everyday effort. The most meaningful surprises are rooted in the little things someone remembers about you.

## 5. The Best-Friend Recognition

Find someone who feels like a best friend, someone you'd instinctively look for in a crowded room and want to tell both your happiest and saddest stories to. Romance should make room for friendship.

## 6. The Rhythm Over the Match

Your tastes don't have to mirror each other, but your emotional rhythms, fundamental values and approaches to life should be compatible. Enjoy common ground without losing your individual identities.

## 7. Healthy Fights

A peaceful relationship doesn't mean a silent one. Disagreements are inevitable; contempt, manipulation, emotional withdrawal and ego-driven battles don't have to be. Learning to disagree without becoming opponents goes a long way.

## 8. Aging & Decay

"The Forever" means holding space for the slow fading of the adventurous body. It means looking past youth, city walks, high energy plans and committing to care for each other through sickness, physical vulnerability and the slow quiet of aging.

## 9. Building a Life Beyond Romance (formerly Cultural, Financial & Political Views)

Love alone cannot sustain a shared life. Cultural expectations, financial habits, political values, family priorities and fundamental beliefs about human dignity must be compatible enough to build a peaceful future.

# II. Friendship & Family Love

The people who make life fuller, give it meaning and remind us that we don't have to navigate it alone.

## 10. Treat People Like Unfinished Artwork

Never assume you've figured someone out completely. People evolve, change their minds and surprise you. Leave room to keep discovering the people you love rather than holding them hostage to an older version of themselves.

## 11. Build a Lighthouse, But Walk Towards One Too

Your people are often found in spaces where they're actively pursuing interests and passions. Find the places where people you admire spend their time, but don't just wait to be discovered. Initiate, participate and build connections.

## 12. The Double-Edged Sword Risk

Opening yourself to meaningful connections also opens you to disappointment and heartbreak. They're a package deal. Don't let the possibility of losing people make you unwilling to welcome them.

## 13. Presence Over Performance

You shouldn't have to constantly entertain people, prove your worth or walk on eggshells to maintain a connection. Be present, make an effort, communicate and allow each other the freedom to have ordinary, uneventful days.

## 14. Accountability Is the Highest Form of Gentleness

The ability to say "I made a mistake, and I'm sorry" without shifting blame or invalidating someone's feelings is essential to every meaningful relationship. Accountability is how trust gets repaired.

## 15. Build Memories, Not Just 10-Year Plans

Stop treating relationships like long-term projects that need predetermined outcomes. Make plans, try things, build traditions and participate in each other's lives. Some of the strongest bonds are built in the present.

## 16. The Healthy Intersection of Social Circles

Healthy relationships should expand your world, not shrink it. Respect each other's friendships, family bonds and individual spaces. The people you love don't need to belong to just one part of your life.

## 17. Sealed in Laughter

Life is too long not to laugh. Find people with whom you can be ridiculous, exchange stupid memes, share inside jokes and turn the most ordinary moments into memorable ones.

## 18. Shut Up and Dance with Me\!

Find people who can navigate life's changing rhythms with you. People who understand that not every step will be perfect and sometimes taking a few steps backwards is still part of a dance together.

## III. Self-Love

Learning to stand on your own, understand yourself and show up for others without losing yourself.

## 19. Self-Love Raises the Bar (And That's a Good Thing)

Respect yourself enough to recognise what you deserve and what compromises your peace. Set boundaries, hold yourself accountable and never shrink your values just to accommodate someone else. But leave room for human imperfection.

## 20. Define Your Readiness

Before looking outward, look inward. Ask yourself what you want from relationships, why you want them and whether you're genuinely ready to accommodate another person's complexities. Become the kind of person you hope to find in others.
- Understand your own emotional needs.
- Recognise when you're seeking reassurance unnecessarily.
- Learn to communicate without expecting people to read your mind.
- Be willing to accommodate someone else's differences, too.

## 21. Share Your Weight, But Never Outsource Your Gravity

Be someone people can lean on, but don't make yourself responsible for fixing their lives. Your healing is yours, just as theirs is theirs. Trust people, accept support & remain independent at your core.

## 22. Words and Actions Must Align

Don't just make promises to yourself or others. Follow through. Practice what you preach, communicate honestly and let your behaviour reflect your values. Hold yourself to the same standards you expect from others.

## 23. Legacy

Every relationship leaves something behind- memories, lessons, habits, values and ways of understanding love. I hope the relationships I build leave behind more kindness, emotional maturity and courage to love openly. Build something meaningful enough that its impact outlasts the moments I've shared with them.


**P.S.** Of course, nobody will embody all these qualities on day one. You don't need perfect people. You need people with the capacity, willingness and desire to grow alongside you.

Keep your high standards. Don't abandon your values. But leave a little room for the chaotic, clumsy, beautifully human ways people try to reach them.

And remember, be the kind of person you hope to find in others.
