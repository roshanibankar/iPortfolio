
## 1. Life Feels Like a Movie Sometimes

I’m sitting in a café writing this post while the song *“My Dil Goes Mmmm”* plays in my headphones. Writing this gives me the classic *romance novelist in a café on a quiet afternoon* vibe. Almost as if this will turn out to be one of those great posts that somehow leads me to meet the love of my life.  

Or maybe he’ll just show up in this café, accidentally drop his coffee on me, and that’s how the fairytale begins : ')

I grew up watching **Romedy Now** during my teenage years and was, obviously, captivated by the endless list of romantic endings. So of course I yearn for my own happy ending. Who wouldn’t?

Maybe that’s why the idea of **“main character energy”** feels so appealing — it turns ordinary moments into scenes from a story.



## 2. The First Time I Felt Like a Main Character

I’m a huge movie buff and a bookworm, which led me to first encounter this idea of being the *“main character”* as a kid when I was lost in the world of stories.

I built really cool hideout spots in my society. I used to gather cardboard boxes and old furniture pieces and cut or arrange them in ways that made them look like a valiant little castle.

Of course, the next day it would all be taken away with the garbage by the society’s helpers.

I also used to climb on car tops and imagine I was stargazing because, come on, even spotting **one star in Bombay (Mumbai now)** felt lucky.

A few years back when I visited it again, I felt life rushing back into me. That’s when I realized the city kind of brought back my *“main character energy”* in a very calming way.

The **city of dreams**, where people go hoping their dreams will come true — and luckily I grew up there.

Not just that, the city also gave me my dream: **becoming an artist**.

I wanted to build the kinds of structures I saw every weekend when I went out with my dad to **VT (now CST)**, **Bandra**, **Andheri**, **South Bombay**, **Kharghar**, and so many other places.  

And slowly, a refined dream of being an **Architect** was born.

So I don’t think the idea of a *“main character”* is something new. Most of us feel it growing up.

And it really is interesting to believe you are the main character of your life, because well… **you are.**



## 3. When Authenticity Becomes a Performance

But the problem begins when we start seeking validation from others to believe that.

The rise of individualism is great, but that doesn’t mean we won’t match outfits, order the same frappe, or choose the same pasta. Sure, you shouldn’t always follow the crowd, but someone choosing the same dish, idea, or opinion as you doesn’t suddenly make them a *“conventional-only”* person.

The present times encourage us to document our lives in creative ways — random grocery store stories, morning routines, small everyday moments.

And many of those posts are genuinely authentic.

But even authentic posts often exist with an **imagined audience** in mind.  
And the moment we imagine an audience, we start shaping the story slightly differently.

When we document life knowing someone is watching, the line between **living** and **performing authenticity** can slowly blur.

In a world where everyone wants to feel significant, valued, and not just ordinary, it’s natural to frame ourselves as the *“main character.”*

Ironically though, that framing can sometimes become a **coping mechanism**.

A little of that energy can boost confidence and turn failures into dramatic comeback scenes.

But too much of it, and suddenly everyone else becomes just an **extra in your story**.

So how do we differentiate?

- How much sharing is too much or too little?
- Who decides?
- And where does real **“main character energy”** fit into all of this?



## 4. How People Documented Life Before Social Media

Sometimes looking at history gives us perspective.

How did people in the past live as the *main characters* of their lives?

They kept **journals**.  
They wrote **letters**.  
They sketched ideas that **no algorithm would ever rank in any feed**.

**Leonardo da Vinci’s notebooks and codices** weren’t curated for followers.  
They were messy conversations with curiosity.

**Vincent van Gogh’s letters** weren’t engagement bait.  
They were raw bridges between brothers.

**Rosalind Franklin’s X-ray images** weren’t crafted for fame — they revealed the blueprint of life itself.

Here’s the catch though:  
she was **never fully recognized for her work while alive.**

Which raises a difficult question:

How many people would build something extraordinary if they knew they might never receive recognition?

The answer becomes easier if the motto is **building things that last beyond you.**



## 5. The Real Form of Authenticity

The most authentic work in history didn’t come from trying to appear interesting.

- A scientist uncovering a law of nature  
- A writer describing human emotions  
- A musician capturing a feeling everyone recognizes  
- A wildlife photographer waiting days in the cold for a snow leopard to arrive  

Aesthetics aren’t the problem.

Street photographers, documentary filmmakers, and scientists carefully frame their shots — but their goal is **revealing something real**, not performing.

Maybe that’s the deeper form of authenticity — not just **sharing life**, but **understanding it**.

And sometimes you see it in love too.

There are moments when the world seems to fall away and someone looks at you like you are the only thing that exists in the room.

People often dismiss grand gestures in love as performative — something done for an audience. And sometimes they are.

But sometimes they aren’t.

Sometimes someone forgets the audience entirely.  
They forget the room, the people, the noise.

And in that moment, the gesture isn’t performance anymore.

It’s just **attention**.

And attention creates its own kind of magic.



Now I look around and see a café moment being staged for photos.  

But then I notice my coffee is getting cold, the song in my headphones is ending, and the café is still humming around me.

Maybe authenticity isn’t a scene after all.

Maybe it’s just **attention**.

Maybe real **main-character energy** isn’t about being seen.

It’s about **seeing the world clearly enough to find something true.**
